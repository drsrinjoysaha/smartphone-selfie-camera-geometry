Smartphone Selfie Camera Geometry: Reproducible Portrait Capture

A technical reference for developers, photographers, and imaging practitioners who need a repeatable smartphone portrait-capture workflow.

Scope: This page covers camera geometry, illumination, device settings, and repeatability. It is not a diagnostic tool, a physical measurement system, or advice about health, appearance, or treatment.

Why close selfies can look different

A close smartphone selfie commonly uses a wide-angle camera. When the camera is close to a three-dimensional subject, nearer regions subtend a larger angle than regions farther away. This can make central regions appear proportionally larger than they do in a more distant capture.

The primary variable is camera-to-subject distance, rather than focal length alone. A longer-equivalent lens is useful because it allows the camera to be placed farther away while retaining practical portrait framing.

Minimal reproducible setup

  • Use a rear camera and a tripod or fixed mount.
  • Use the same device, camera module, capture mode, and output resolution.
  • Place camera and subject at repeatable marked positions.
  • Use a neutral matte background and one stable light source.
  • Disable beauty filters, face reshaping, smoothing, and comparable effects.

Camera geometry

Distance

Start with a camera-to-subject distance of approximately 1.5–2.5 metres for head-and-shoulders framing, then document the distance actually used. Keep it fixed for later captures.

Lens selection

When available, choose a rear-camera mode offering approximately 50–85 mm full-frame-equivalent framing, often labelled 2× or 3× on a smartphone. Avoid digital zoom if a native camera module is available.

Focus, exposure, and illumination

  • Focus near the subject plane and lock focus where supported.
  • Set and lock exposure where supported.
  • Use one stable light source; avoid mixed daylight and indoor lighting.
  • Keep light position and colour rendering consistent across sessions.

Views and metadata

With the camera fixed, capture front, left/right 45-degree oblique, and left/right 90-degree profile views. Keep camera height, distance, lens selection, and lighting unchanged.

Preserve original image files and record device, camera module, zoom mode, camera distance, camera height, view, lighting, and processing settings.

Limitations

Smartphone photographs are rendered two-dimensional images, not calibrated physical models. Device-specific optics, computational processing, pose, focus, lighting, compression, and display pipelines can all affect results. Do not infer real-world dimensions, health status, or outcomes from uncalibrated consumer-camera imagery.
